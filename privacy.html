Tin Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Tin ("the app") helps you look around a room. It marks small bright reflections in the camera view, lists nearby Bluetooth devices that are broadcasting, and lists devices on your local network that advertise a service. This policy explains what stays on your iPhone and what does not.

What the app does not collect

Tin does not have an account system and does not send camera images, Bluetooth scan results, or local-network scan results to us or to any server we operate. Those results stay on your iPhone.

  • Camera frames are processed on the device to mark small bright spots. They are not uploaded. A photo is saved to your photo library only when you tap save.
  • Bluetooth names and signal strength, and local-network device names, addresses, and response times, are shown on screen and are not uploaded.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not use your data for advertising or tracking.

Permissions

  • Camera — to show the view and mark small reflections, such as those from a lens.
  • Bluetooth — to list nearby devices that are broadcasting.
  • Local Network — to find devices on your Wi-Fi that advertise a service and to measure a response time.
  • Location — iOS requires this permission before an app can display the name of the Wi-Fi network you are connected to. Tin does not track your location.
  • Photos — only to save a photo you choose to keep.

Purchases

Subscriptions and the 7-day pass are sold and billed by Apple through the App Store. Tin does not receive or store your payment details. Apple provides the app with the status of your purchase so it can unlock features. Apple's privacy policy applies to that payment.
